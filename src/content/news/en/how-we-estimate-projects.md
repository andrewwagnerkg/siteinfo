A cost estimate without a written scope is a guess. These four steps turn it into a plan that both sides can check.

## 1. Describe goals and users

Start with the business goal and the people who will use the product. "Customers can book a visit online in under a minute" is a goal. "A booking module" is not.

## 2. Split the work into features and estimate in ranges

Break the product into features small enough to estimate: a few days each. Give each feature a range, for example "3–5 days", not a single number. The width of the range shows how much is still unknown.

## 3. Write down risks and assumptions

Integrations with external systems, unclear requirements and new technologies are the usual sources of delay. List them next to the estimate, together with what you assumed, for example "the payment provider has a test environment".

## 4. Agree on the first release and review the estimate

Choose the smallest version of the product that already brings value, and estimate it first. After every iteration, compare the plan with reality and update the estimate for the rest of the work.
